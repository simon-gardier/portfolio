<Callout type="danger" title="Before continuing, be aware!!">
  1) The freedom and money you get from owning your own server definitely does not compensate for: the monetary cost of

(maybe they could have found 2 if they added "make no mistake"). These agents are making automated (/directed) attacks easier than ever.
  The guide will provide you with basics security recommendations but it is definitely not a full cybersecurity course.

  3) Last but not least, self hosting you own stack of apps is super duper fun and you will enjoy it and will not be able to stop!!!!

### Access
By default, to access your apps you would have to enter your server IP, do port forwarding etc... We don't want/like that!
Instead go the easy way, buy a domain (e.g. yourdomain.com) and make your services available at this address.
In the article we will organise the apps in subdomains, so Nextcloud will be available at nextcloud.yourdomain.com, Immich at immich.yourdomain.com etc, you get it.

To direct our subdomain requests to the correct app, we will use a reverse proxy ([Caddy](https://caddyserver.com/docs/quick-starts/reverse-proxy)).
The reverse proxy is the software that resolves which app should handle incomming traffic based on the URL of your request. Here is how it works:
- You type an URL in your browser (e.g. immich.yourdomain.com)
- Your request reaches your server (thanks internet, DHCP, etc...)
- The request is forwarded to your reverse proxy device app an url 

<ArticleImage src="/images/blog/self-hosting/reverse_proxy.png" alt="Reverse proxy illustration" caption="Reverse proxy in a nutshell. The software receives subdomains requests and forward them to the concerned app." />

Another free improvement the reverse proxy will provide is [HTTPS](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertext_Transfer_Protocol_Secure), which is required for some apps. Not using HTTPs would like surfing naked on the internet. HTTPs encrypt our data such that only sender and receiver can read it.

### Safe access
To avoid having bots/bad actors (not the Hollywood ones) accessing your apps, you will have to implement multiple practices.
The first one is to gate the access to the apps behind a [VPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_private_network).
A VPN creates a private network on which only allowed devices can communicate together / access ressources present on the private network.

### Backups
Self hosting a cloud service or as photo service will require you to considerate backup solutions. You certainly do not want to be the
person responsible for the loss of the entire family photon album, right? Then do backups!

This section will not be covered in this article as it merits its own. In the mean time, take a look at solutions like [Restic](https://restic.net/) and related tools like [Backrest](https://github.com/garethgeorge/backrest)

## Hardware

Now that we have an idea of what can be done let's choose the hardware. The sky is the limit in terms of budget but here are some populars choices:

| Computer | Budget (without hard drives) | Main selling point(s) | Main drawback | Link to product (not sponsored) |
| --------| ------------- | ------------- | -------------- | ---------- |
| Raspberry Pi 5 (or 4) | 200€ | Low budget, very low power consumption, small, silent, self hosting very well documented on the internet | Hardware acceleration for video encoding is not supported anymore in Jellyfin, making it not well fitted for this app | [raspberrypi.com](https://www.raspberrypi.com/products/raspberry-pi-5/) | 
| NUC computer | 200-350€ | Low budget Intel computer, their processors are supported for hardware acceleration by Jellyfin | Example: [ASUS NUC 14](https://www.amazon.com.be/-/en/ASUS-Essential-RNUC14MNK1500002-processor-Compatible/dp/B0DX6X9H3G/ref=sr_1_5?sr=8-5) |
| Mini Intel PC | 250-500€ | Middle budget Intel computer. 
| PC at home | 0€ | The best choice if you are starting! You are broke and/or you have a spare PC at home | 

While the Raspberry Pi 5 is not recommended for Jellyfin it is *technically* feasible.
In fact, I used it as my home server for more than a year. The main drawback that you will have to stream your movies at 1.5Mbps at most (i.e. low quality) and 
that watch parties will not be feasible as it is simply too much work for the Raspberry Pi.
Another issue is that the 8GB of ram get quickly exhausted if multiple applications are installed and running at the same time.

If you have the budget, dont care that much about electricity bill and want a no brainer choice, go for:
- CPU: Any Intel i5/i7/i9 you can find of generation >= 11th
- RAM: At least 16GB, Ideally 24/32GB
- Drives: 1 SSD for the OS (capacity >= 512GB), 4 HDD for your apps data (1TB each), 2 HDD for backups (1TB each).
- The other components dont matter, anything compatible will do the work, use [pcpartpicker.com](https://pcpartpicker.com/) to find compatible hardware. 

## Operating System

If you have not real experience with System Administration and Linux (and in any other case), I would recommend to install Ubuntu os the OS for your server.
A guide to install it can be found here: [Install Ubuntu Desktop
](https://ubuntu.com/tutorials/install-ubuntu-desktop#1-overview). The desktop version is fine and it will be easier to use if you are new.
You can also go for the [Server](https://ubuntu.com/download/server) version but you have to be confortable with using the terminal. This options have the advantage of leaving more RAM and disk space for your applications.
